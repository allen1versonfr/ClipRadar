Clip Radar
==========
Turns a Twitch VOD into vertical TikTok clips. It finds the best moments,
lets you review and trim them, then renders 1080x1920 MP4s with captions.
Everything runs on your own PC.

INSTALL
  Download ClipRadar-Setup.exe from the repo's Releases page and run it.
  It installs Clip Radar for your user (no admin prompt) and adds Start menu
  and desktop shortcuts. Windows may show a SmartScreen warning because the
  app isn't signed: More info > Run anyway.

  No-install option: ClipRadar-portable.zip. Unzip anywhere and run
  ClipRadar.exe. To remove it, delete the folder.

UPDATES
  Clip Radar checks GitHub for a new version when it starts (and every few
  hours). When there is one, "Update to x.y.z" appears in the status bar.
  Settings > Updates shows what's new; Update now downloads it, closes
  Clip Radar, installs it and opens Clip Radar again. Your clips, projects and
  settings are kept. Turn off "Check for updates automatically" there if you
  prefer to check yourself. The portable version links to the download page.
  Settings > Updates > All versions lists every version with what changed.
  Install any version newer than yours from there; older versions are kept
  on GitHub's Releases page as an archive (with their source code).
  Clip Radar was called VodClipper before version 4.0. Updating VodClipper
  in the app installs Clip Radar over it: the old program folder and
  shortcuts go, and on first start your settings move to
  %LOCALAPPDATA%\ClipRadar and your clips folder to Videos\Clip Radar,
  with everything in Exports and your projects still in place.
  If Clip Radar's GitHub repository is private, the updater needs a read-only
  access token: Settings > Updates > Private repository explains how to make
  one. It's stored encrypted for your Windows account.

UNINSTALL
  Any of these:
    - Settings > Apps > Installed apps > Clip Radar > Uninstall
    - Start menu > Uninstall Clip Radar
    - In Clip Radar: Settings > About > Uninstall
  It removes the app, its downloaded tools, caption models and logs, then asks
  whether to delete your VODs and clips too (it keeps them unless you say Yes).

BUILD IT YOURSELF (optional)
  Every push to the repo builds and tests the installer on Windows (GitHub
  Actions), and every version tag publishes it as a release. To build on your
  own PC instead, run scripts\build_exe.bat from the source code.

USE
  Clip Radar has four sections in the left sidebar: Home, Review, Exports and
  Settings. The status bar at the bottom shows what's running in the background.

  Home: start a project
    Past broadcast   paste a link or Browse to a video on your PC, then Find
                     clips. Links can be a Twitch VOD (twitch.tv/videos/...), a
                     YouTube video, Short or finished livestream, or an X post
                     with a video. Clip Radar says what the link is first
                     (an ordinary video, a finished livestream, or one that's
                     live now) and explains in plain words when it can't be
                     used: private, age-restricted or members-only (choose
                     your browser under Advanced > Sign in with), removed,
                     not available in your country, or a post with no video.
                     A finished YouTube livestream brings its chat replay, so
                     chat counts like it does for Twitch (YouTube emoji
                     included). An ordinary YouTube video has no chat, so Clip
                     Radar reads what YouTube knows about it: the Most replayed
                     graph (where viewers rewatch), timestamps in the top
                     comments ("3:45 when he fell": many people pointing at a
                     moment lifts it, and their words can title the clip, shown
                     as Comments: "..."), chapters, and YouTube's own captions,
                     which title clips straight away without waiting for
                     Whisper. Each clip's reason says which of these it came
                     from. A link with a start
                     time (?t=1m30s) opens the preview there. Each clip has a
                     link back to that moment in the original (Clip tab, and
                     Copy source link in Exports) for crediting the source.
                     Clips to suggest and Clip length are Automatic by default:
                     the number scales with how long the stream is and how much
                     happened, and each clip gets its own length (15-90 s).
                     Deep scan (optional) also transcribes the whole stream to
                     find quiet gems (see below).
    Live stream      enter a Twitch channel (twitch.tv/name) or a YouTube
                     livestream link and press Start live clipping while the
                     stream is on air. A YouTube livestream's chat is read too
                     (through yt-dlp), the same way as Twitch chat.
    Running now      every video being opened and every stream being clipped,
                     with its progress. Open, Pause (Resume carries on where the
                     download stopped), Cancel or Stop each one by itself; one
                     failing doesn't affect the others. You can open another
                     video while one is still being opened, and clip up to
                     three streams at once (Settings > Performance > Live
                     streams at once; two by default). Each source keeps its
                     own folder, chat, captions and clip numbers.

  Review: decide what to keep
    The suggested clips are listed on the left. The middle shows the selected
    clip exactly as the TikTok will look (layout, title, word-by-word captions).
    The inspector on the right has four tabs:
      Clip       title, post caption and trim
      Captions   every caption line with its time. Click a line to jump to it,
                 type over it to fix a word (Enter to save), x to delete it,
                 or Add caption at playhead to write your own. Undo all edits
                 brings back what Whisper heard.
      Text       caption style (Pop, Highlight, Karaoke, Glow, Clean), title
                 style (white, dark or colour box, outline), fonts, size and
                 highlight colour
      Layout     shape (vertical 9:16, square 1:1, wide 16:9), cam + game,
                 whole frame, cam only or blur fit, facecam box, focus, zoom,
                 follow the streamer's face, saved framings
    Drag the title or the captions on the preview to move them; a line shows
    when they're centred, and the shaded areas are covered by TikTok's own
    buttons. Text > Reset positions puts them back.
    The hype timeline along the bottom shows the whole stream; click a
    highlighted range to jump to that clip, or (for VODs) click anywhere else to
    preview that moment and make a clip there.
      Keep and render (Enter)   renders it in the background
      Skip (X)                  sets it aside (Skipped tab)
    The header shows how many strong moments the stream has (for example
    "about 21 strong moments in this 7-hour stream"). What you keep, skip and
    post teaches Clip Radar what you like for each streamer; after about eight
    decisions the header says "Tuned to your picks" (Settings > Clip ranking).
    Gems tab (after a Deep scan): quiet gems, lines that landed (the streamer
    laughed after, chat reacted, the voice changed, a punchline) even though
    chat and volume stayed calm. Deep scan can also be started from the
    header, and you can keep reviewing while it runs.
      Up / Down arrows          previous / next clip
      Space play/pause, T new title, M mute, Left/Right seek, C clip (live)
    Editing timeline (under the player): the clip's stretch of the stream
    with its sound, captions, laughter, screams and cheers, chat activity and
    content check flags. Drag the clip's edges to trim (they snap to word
    edges; hold Alt to stop that), click to jump, scroll to move along and
    Ctrl+scroll (or - Fit +) to zoom. Times are stream times.
    Trim in the Clip tab by a second (-1s/+1s) or a single frame (-1f/+1f), or
    press I and O to start or end the clip at the playhead. Restore original
    cut goes back to the cut Clip Radar found. Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z,
    Ctrl+Shift+Z) cover trims, titles, post captions and caption edits.
    Frame back/forward (, and .), playback speed (< and >), and ? shows every
    shortcut.
    Exact preview renders a few seconds around the playhead exactly like the
    export (captions, title, censoring, shape), without exporting the clip.
    Each clip has a number (#7) that stays with that moment, even when you
    change the sliders. Clips you kept, skipped, trimmed or retitled never drop
    out of the list. Your review is saved as you go: open the same VOD again
    (Recent on the Home screen) and everything is where you left it.

  Live mode
    Pick a mode on the Home screen:
      Hands-free         every moment is titled, checked and rendered by itself
                         with your Text and Layout style. Finished clips appear
                         in Exports while you watch. (Moments the content check
                         flags as serious are held back for you in Review.)
      Review each clip   moments wait in Review; you keep the ones you want.
    How many clips: Fewer, Normal, More or Lots. Lowest score: moments under
    it aren't clipped (50 is a clear moment, 75 a strong one). You can change
    both while live.
    While live, the line under the header shows a moment as it forms, how
    late chat reacts (worked out from the stream unless you set Chat delay),
    how long each step takes and about how many seconds after something
    happens its clip is ready, and how many moments were passed on (hover
    for why: under your lowest score, only one or two people in chat,
    unlike your picks, or a repeat of a moment already clipped).
    If chat disconnects or goes quiet, moments are found from the stream's
    sound until chat is back. When several moments wait to be captioned or
    rendered, a clip you asked for goes first, then the strongest.
    Wait for the stream to start: start before the channel goes live and
    recording begins by itself. Keep watching: after the stream ends,
    Clip Radar waits for the channel's next stream and starts again.
      Ctrl+Alt+C          clip the last 30 seconds from ANY window (a sound
                          confirms it). Turn it off in Settings > Live clipping.
      Clip last 30s (C)   the same, from inside Clip Radar
      Stop                stop recording; you can keep reviewing afterwards
    Only the last 15 minutes of raw stream are kept on disk; moments are cut out
    as they happen, so long streams don't fill your drive. If the connection
    drops it reconnects, and it notices when the stream ends.
    Ads: Twitch stitches ads into live streams for viewers without Turbo.
    Clip Radar recognises those ad breaks and leaves them out of the recording,
    so they never end up in a clip (the header shows "Ads skipped"). Sign in
    under Settings > Twitch sign-in with an account that has Turbo (or a sub to
    the channel) and Twitch sends the ad-free stream instead.

  Exports: finished clips and posting
    Every kept clip renders here, named after its title, with its post caption
    saved next to it as a .txt. Select one to watch it and post it to TikTok.
    If a clip fails to render, its row says why (for example "your drive is
    full") and what to do, with a Retry button. Retry failed retries them all.
    A retry replaces its own file instead of making a "(2)" copy, and every
    finished file is checked before it shows as done. Exports and any renders
    that hadn't finished are kept when you close Clip Radar; unfinished
    renders start again the next time you open it.

CONTENT CHECK (Settings > Content check)
  Checks what each clip says, its title and its post caption for things that
  get TikTok videos removed or held back: hate slurs, self-harm and
  harassment, personal info (phone numbers, emails, street addresses),
  violence, sexual terms, drugs, swearing, gambling, and your own blocked
  words (your real name, your town...). Flags show on each clip.
  When something is flagged: just flag it, hide it in captions ("f***"),
  mute it, or bleep it. Choose which kinds get censored in the Censor column.
  It reads words only. It can't see the picture or recognise music.

  Reviewing flags
    A flagged clip shows a Content check box in Review, listing each flag
    with what was said and when (click the time to hear it). For each flag:
      Mute it / Bleep it      silence or bleep just those words when it renders
      End the clip before it / Start the clip after it   move the cut
      Hide it in the title    replace the words with stars
      Dismiss                 a false alarm, or not a problem for you
    A fix explains what it will change first; Preview plays it, and Undo fix
    takes it back. Your video itself is never changed.
    For the whole clip: Leave as is (keep it exactly as it is), Review and
    edit (jumps to the words in the Captions tab), Exclude from export (stays
    in the project but won't render until you include it again), and
    Remove (takes it out of the project for good; Undo brings it back).
    Every clip gets a status: Needs review, Reviewed, Corrected, Dismissed
    or Excluded from export. The Flagged tab lists flagged clips with a
    status filter and Leave all / Dismiss all / Exclude all.
    A decision sticks to that flag: it doesn't come back by itself, but it
    does if the words change, if you press Check again from scratch, or if
    you change what the check looks for. History on each clip lists what was
    decided. Decisions are saved with the VOD.
    Dismissing or leaving a flag never means a clip is safe. This is
    Clip Radar's own check, not TikTok's decision.

  Settings for reviewing
    Sensitivity: Everything, Skip mild (no swearing or gambling) or Only
    serious. Check clips by themselves (or press Check now on each clip).
    Show flags right away, or collect them in the Flagged tab. Rendering a
    clip with flags you haven't reviewed: Ask me first, Not until reviewed,
    or Just render it. Remember flags I dismiss. Check a clip again after I
    edit or fix it.

  Auto titles and captions
    Clip Radar listens to each clip (Whisper) and reads how chat reacted, then
    writes two titles:
      A  the best line said near the peak, or a short line about how chat
         reacted (laughing, hyped, shocked...)
      B  the same idea with the streamer's name worked in
    Both show under the title box; click one to use it. The refresh button
    (or T) cycles through more ideas, and you can type your own. Titles only
    describe what's in the clip: what was said and how chat reacted.
    The streamer's name comes from the chat replay; "Add the streamer's name"
    (or "change") sets how it's written, for example JasonTheWeen.
    The post caption is written separately from the title, with up to five
    hashtags that fit the clip. The Hashtags switch under it turns tags off
    for every clip. Settings > Titles and captions picks which title new clips
    use (A or B) and whether to add hashtags.
    Titles from what chat said: when at least three different chatters type
    the same short phrase about a moment, it's offered as a title, shown as
    Chat: "...". One viewer spamming, emote walls, F's, links, numbers and
    usernames never count, and a strong line said on stream still comes first.

POSTING TO TIKTOK
  You post in TikTok Studio, TikTok's own upload page; nothing to set up.
  In Exports, select a clip, check the caption, and press Open in TikTok
  Studio. The caption (with your post hashtags) is copied, the video is
  selected in File Explorer so you can drag it into the page, and TikTok
  Studio opens in your browser. Finish uploading and posting there.
  Clip Radar can't see your browser, so it only says it opened TikTok Studio,
  never that a clip was posted.

HASHTAGS
  Settings > Hashtags has two separate lists:
    In file names: the real tag text, # included, added to each exported
    file's name, like Chat lost it #KaiCenat #Mafia #TwitchClips.mp4. The
    streamer's own tag, your tags for that streamer (one per line,
    "Kai Cenat: #KaiCenat #Mafia"), then your general tags, no repeats, up
    to the number you set. Long names drop tags from the end first. A retry
    keeps the same name.
    In post captions: Clip Radar's tags for each clip (platform, streamer,
    mood), your tags for every post, for a streamer and for each platform.
  The preview shows both for any streamer name.

STORAGE FOLDERS
  Settings > Storage lists where each kind of file goes, with the free space
  on its drive. Change… picks another folder; Default goes back.
    Projects                 a folder per video: your review, analysis,
                             transcripts and thumbnails
    Downloaded videos        downloaded VODs and videos (default: inside each
                             project folder)
    Rendered clips           finished clips (default: a "tiktok" folder inside
                             each project folder)
    Temporary files          exact previews, framing data and the live
                             recording buffer. Clear empties it; it never
                             touches your videos, projects or clips.
    Logs                     clipradar.log, and errors.jsonl (every failure)
  A folder is checked before it's used (it must exist or be creatable, and be
  writable). New work goes to the new folder; nothing already saved is moved,
  and projects in a previous Projects folder still show up. A folder on a
  drive that isn't plugged in falls back to the default until it's back, and
  Clip Radar says so. Downloads stop before starting if the drive is too full,
  and a second render at once waits while memory is short.

FREEING UP SPACE
  Downloaded VODs are big (several GB per stream). Settings > Storage shows
  how much space Clip Radar is using:
    Delete VODs       removes downloaded streams (and their chat/thumbnails).
                      Your rendered clips are kept. Videos of your own that you
                      opened with Browse are never deleted.
    Delete all clips  removes every rendered TikTok clip. VODs are kept.
  Both ask for confirmation first.
  Clean up automatically: old temporary files (on); a hands-free clip's file
  when you skip it (off); and a downloaded VOD once you're finished with it
  (off): every clip kept or skipped, every kept clip rendered, nothing waiting
  or failed, and not opened for the days you choose. Rendered clips and your
  review are never removed. The last run, and anything it couldn't remove,
  shows there.

HOW CLIPS ARE PICKED
  Every second of the stream gets a score from several signals, each measured
  against what's normal for this stream (so big and small channels are judged
  against themselves):
  - Chat: sudden surges in how many people are chatting, weighted for KEKW,
    LUL, POG, "clip it", ????? and similar, and how fast it speeds up. It counts
    chatters, not messages (one person spamming counts once), and ignores bots,
    !commands and giveaway entries, sub and gift-sub notices, copypasta, and the
    floods after gift bombs and raids.
  - Emotes: waves of 7TV, BetterTTV and FrankerFaceZ emotes (and Twitch ones)
    from many chatters at once. The channel's emote names are downloaded and
    cached for a day (Advanced > Recognise emotes); without them it carries on.
  - Audio: moments much louder than the few minutes around them. Twitch's
    muted (DMCA) sections are ignored instead of counted as boring. A sudden
    reaction right after a quiet stretch counts extra; a short loud blip that
    nobody reacted to (a door, an alert sound) counts less.
  - Laughter, screams and cheering, heard by a small built-in sound detector
    (Advanced > Listen for laughter, on by default; it adds about a minute per
    hour of video). Loud music counts for less.
  - Chat delay: chat reacts a few seconds late. Clip Radar works out how late
    from the stream itself (leave Advanced > Chat delay empty), or uses the
    number you type.
  - Where a clip starts and ends: far enough back to show the setup (and,
    when chat reacted late, what it was reacting to), on until the reaction
    dies down, and moved to the nearest pause between sentences once the clip
    has been listened to. Two detections of the same moment become one clip.
  - Stream type (Gaming, IRL / Just Chatting, Reactions, Interviews, Podcasts
    or Any stream) changes how much each signal counts and the clip lengths.
    How many moments (Fewer, Normal, More) sets how strong a moment has to be.
    Shortest and longest clip are under Advanced.
  - Your own keeps, skips and posts, per streamer, and where you trim clips:
    after five trims, new clips start and end where you usually move them to
    (Settings > Clip ranking, where you can also make it forget).
  - With no chat log it ranks by audio and the sound detector only.
  - Local video? Put its chat next to it as <videoname>.chat.json, or choose
    the file under Home > Past broadcast > Advanced. TwitchDownloader chat JSON works.

  Scores and settings
    Every clip gets a score from 0 to 100 (50 is a clear moment, 75 a strong
    one) and a breakdown of what made it: chat, volume, laughter, emotes and so
    on. Scores rank moments within one stream; they can't promise views. 90+
    is kept for the stream's best few (at most 5) and 75+ for its top quarter
    (at most 20), so a long stream with 60 clips doesn't grade them all 90+.
    The detection button in the Review header shows the settings in use. Click
    it to change them: Try shows how many clips would be new, dropped or the
    same before you apply anything, and clips you kept, skipped, trimmed or
    added stay either way. The settings are saved with the VOD.
    After you skip a clip, you can say why (not interesting, bad cut,
    duplicate, just noise). Settings > Clip ranking shows how many suggestions
    you keep, why you skip, and how many moments you had to add by hand.
    What it learned (per streamer) shows exactly what's stored, how the clips
    you kept differed from the ones you skipped, what ranking moves up or down
    because of it, and the rules every clip is scored on. The learning is a
    small statistical model worked out on your PC, not an AI, and it says when
    it doesn't have enough decisions yet.
  - A wave of "F" (or o7, or poll votes like "1") counts for nothing: it says
    something happened, not what, and can't make a clip on its own.
  - Keep the top (To review list): keep and render the top 3, 5, 10, 15, 20
    or all clips in one go, in the order shown, with your export settings.
    Clips with content flags to review are left for you.
  - Skip (To review list): skip every clip left in the list, or only those
    scored under 40, 50, 60 or 75. Clips you made yourself stay. Undo brings
    them back, and skipped clips can still be kept from the Skipped tab.
  - Clips to suggest goes from 5 to 60 in steps of 5, then up to 100. You get
    the number you asked for; when a video doesn't have that many separate
    moments, Clip Radar says so.

FRAMING
  Layout > Auto-frame looks at a few frames of the video and picks a layout:
  a face that sits still in a corner is a facecam (Cam + game, with the box
  drawn around the camera), a person filling the frame is followed (Whole
  frame), and with no face it shows the middle of the frame. Check the box
  it draws; you can always drag your own.
  Follow the streamer's face (Whole frame layout): the crop moves with the
  face, smoothly and never faster than a slow pan, ignores small wobbles and
  one-off misdetections, and holds still while the face is hidden for a
  moment. It moves to someone else only once the person it follows has been
  gone for a second and a half. If the face is gone for longer it eases back to Focus. If a face is
  only seen now and then, or the only face is a facecam overlay, the crop
  stays at Focus. Who to follow: automatic (the biggest face, or two people
  together when they fit in the frame side by side), the left-most
  or right-most person, or the one nearest a focus point you set. Captions in
  the automatic position move off the face. The preview moves the crop the
  same way, and the exact preview shows the real render.
  Cam only fills the frame with the facecam. Two or more people on camera?
  Add a facecam (up to four) and drag a box around each: in Cam + game they
  sit side by side above the game, in Cam only one above the other. Saved
  framings keep a layout, the facecam boxes, focus, zoom and follow settings
  to reuse in one click.

  Clips that are already cut
    Home › The video is › "Already a clip" opens the whole video (up to 15
    minutes) as one clip in Review: add captions, pick a layout, Keep and
    render. "Automatic" does this by itself for an X video under 3 minutes
    with no chat; pick "A stream or long video" to find moments in it instead.
  Faces are found by a small built-in detector (UltraFace, MIT licence); it
  reads a few small frames per second, so following adds a few seconds to a
  render.

CAPTIONS
  Made with Whisper. The first render with captions downloads the model
  (a few hundred MB). "Balanced" accuracy is a solid default. With an NVIDIA GPU and
  CUDA 12 + cuDNN installed, Whisper uses the GPU; otherwise the CPU.
  Rendering uses your GPU's video encoder (NVIDIA, Intel or AMD) when it can,
  and falls back to the CPU on its own.
  Whisper is told the streamer's name, the channel's emotes and your own
  list (Settings > Caption words > Words to recognise), so they're spelled
  right. Lines it invents over silence or music ("thanks for watching") are
  dropped. Replace words swaps a word everywhere in captions (leave the
  replacement empty to remove it); timing stays the same.
  The Text tab has Outline and Shadow sliders, Templates (Signature yellow,
  Karaoke, Boxed, Bold bounce, Clean subtitles, Word by word), and Saved
  styles: save the look of your captions and title, then apply it to any clip
  in one click. Reset to default puts the style back without moving anything.
  Animation: pop in, word pop, bounce, fade in, slide up, word by word, or
  none, with a speed setting. Highlight strong words keeps words like "no
  way" and shouting in your highlight colour. Keep "um" and "uh" and Keep
  punctuation can be turned off; the timing of the other words doesn't change.
  Line spacing spreads the two lines of a long caption apart. Timing moves
  every caption earlier or later (up to a second) if they don't line up with
  the voice.
  Show what TikTok covers shades the parts TikTok's buttons, caption and top
  bar sit on, so you can keep text clear of them.

EXPORT FORMAT
  Settings > Export format picks a preset: TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram
  Reels or X. Each sets the size, quality, sound and the longest
  clip the platform takes (a longer clip gets a warning in Exports). The
  platforms change their limits now and then, so treat these as starting
  points. Advanced changes resolution, frame rate, codec (H.264, H.265, or
  VP9 in a .webm), quality or bitrate, audio bitrate and container; Reset
  goes back to the preset. The shape (9:16, 1:1, 16:9) stays in the Layout tab.
  Captions: burned in, burned in plus a .srt file, or only a .srt file.
  Sound: every clip is evened out to the preset's loudness (-14 LUFS by
  default, or left as is). Reduce background noise and Clearer voice are
  optional. A clip that comes out without sound is rendered again; one that
  comes out almost silent gets a warning.
  File names: a template such as {streamer} - {number} - {title}, with
  {platform}, {source}, {date}, {hashtags}, {project} and {preset} too.
  Empty fields drop out, and characters Windows can't use are removed.
  The same clip sent twice (a double click) renders once.
  Quality: Fast export (about a third quicker, up to 30 FPS), Balanced
  (recommended) and High quality (sharper, about 1.5 times as long to render).
  Balanced and High keep the stream's own frame rate, up to 60. A 30 FPS
  stream is never turned into 60: that would only repeat frames, not add
  motion.
  Watermark: up to 6 layers, each text or an image. Text layers have a font,
  colour, outline or a box behind them (in any colour). Every layer has a
  place (a corner, the centre, or Anywhere: drag it on the clip preview in
  Review), size, opacity, rotation, distance from the side, and when it
  appears and disappears. Only exports get it; your videos aren't changed.
  Corners sit below TikTok's top bar or above its caption area.
  Save as... keeps a set of layers under a name. Share... saves one file
  with the layers and their images (in the projects folder's Shared folder)
  to drop into Discord. Import... and From link... bring in a watermark or
  campaign file, or a logo image, from your PC or a Discord link.
  Volume (Clip tab): make one clip quieter or louder than the rest.
  A sound plays when you add a clip to the render queue and when it's ready.

CAMPAIGNS (CLIPPING ORGANISATIONS AND DISCORDS)
  Settings > Campaigns keeps each clipping campaign's rules together:
  - the text every post must carry (an @handle, a code, #ad), at the end or
    the start of the post caption, and hashtags it must carry (added on top
    of yours and never left out);
  - the shortest and longest clip it accepts (clips outside get a warning in
    Exports);
  - its own watermark (Copy my current watermark, then edit it under Export
    format > Watermark while the campaign is active);
  - its Discord channel: paste the channel's webhook link (channel settings >
    Integrations > Webhooks > Copy webhook URL) and press Test.
  Pick the Active campaign: clips you keep from then on follow its rules, and
  Exports shows which campaign is active. In Exports, Submit to <campaign>
  sends a finished clip to its Discord channel: the video (up to 10 MB) until
  you paste the link to your post, then the link. Or turn on Send each clip
  as soon as it renders. The message can use {title}, {caption}, {post_url},
  {campaign}, {source} and {file}; it never pings anyone.
  Share... saves the campaign as one file (rules and watermark) to hand to
  clippers. The webhook is left out unless you tick Include the webhook:
  anyone with it can post in that channel.

PERFORMANCE
  Settings > Performance sets how many clips render at once (1-3) and how
  many VODs download at once (1-2). Keep the PC responsive renders at lower
  priority and leaves one CPU core free; Finish faster uses everything.
  Time left shows in Exports, on Home and in the status bar. It's based on
  how fast this PC rendered before, so the first render's estimate firms up
  after a few seconds.

TROUBLESHOOTING
  - Twitch downloads suddenly fail: Settings > Downloads and tools > Update downloader.
    No rebuild needed.
  - Subscriber-only VOD: sign in under Settings > Twitch sign-in. (Or, the older
    way: Settings > Downloads and tools > Use cookies from > the browser where
    you're logged in to Twitch; close that browser first.)
  - Ads still pause live recordings: that's expected without Turbo or a sub;
    the ads are left out of the clips either way.
  - The window doesn't open: Clip Radar falls back to your web browser.
    Installing Microsoft's "WebView2 Runtime" brings the app window back.
  - Preview won't play: some files (MKV, HEVC) can't play in the preview.
    Finding and rendering clips still works.
  - Something else broke: send the log files at
    %LOCALAPPDATA%\ClipRadar\clipradar.log and errors.jsonl. errors.jsonl
    lists every failure with what Clip Radar was doing (Settings › About ›
    Error log: Open logs folder, or Copy recent errors).

Only clip VODs you own or have permission to repost.
