# The VODs scripts/eval_ranking.py scores Clip Radar's ranking on (docs/plans/ranking-eval.md, section 5).
#
#   python scripts/eval_ranking.py build CORPUS --write-manifest     # download and freeze them (CORPUS outside the repo)
#   python scripts/eval_ranking.py score CORPUS --out rank_out
#
# One VOD a line: VOD_ID[@START-END] [key=value ...] [holdout]  # note
#   @START-END   the stretch, in seconds. Left out, build picks one at random (seeded by the VOD's id, so the same
#                every time): the whole VOD when it's under 3 hours, otherwise 60 to 120 minutes, always where every
#                clip viewers made is known. build --write-manifest fills it in, with the build date and the counts.
#   cat=         chatting, variety (games other than shooters), irl, fps, events. From the VOD's own category.
#   size=        small (under 2,000 watching), medium (2,000 to 15,000), big (over 15,000), live on 2026-11-17.
#   lang=        the stream's language.
#   holdout      a third of each category (at least one left in), picked at random (seed 97). score leaves these out unless --holdout:
#                look at them only when a change is otherwise ready, so changes aren't tuned to the VODs they're
#                checked on (section 8).
#
# Chosen 2026-11-17 from 1,523 public VODs surveyed across categories and languages: these are VODs that have at
# least 8 moments two or more people clipped (or 500+ views) in their stretch, at most two per channel. That rule
# leaves out most small streams and most shooter VODs, so they're under-represented: few people clip them. Also left
# out: subscriber-only VODs, and VODs with parts Twitch muted for music (yt-dlp's sections come down empty from
# those), which keeps out music-heavy streams. 8 of the first 30 picked were one or the other, so there's 1 shooter
# VOD and 1 French one. The clips come from each VOD's own list (both ends of it, 100 each), not the channel's most
# viewed clips that the app reads (twitchclips.fetch), which on most VODs is a small part of them. A clip ends at
# Twitch's videoOffsetSeconds; it doesn't start there (measured on 22 clips, see eval_ranking.vod_clips).
#
# Twitch deletes VODs after 7 to 60 days, and with them the video and chat (not the clips). Build soon after a change
# here; a VOD that's gone can't be built again, so keep the CORPUS folder.
